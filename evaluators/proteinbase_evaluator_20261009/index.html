Inspect a test

What was actually held out?

Within the same antigen

Related sequences, recognizable parent lineages, and shared embedded domains stay in the same fold. Each protein is predicted by a model that did not train on its family.

EGFR is the difficult test: only 9 groups contain binders; two large groups contain 46 of its 53 binders. These groups remain intact.

Across antigens

Train on EGFR and test on TREM2, then reverse the direction. Shared families are removed from the training antigen: 62 EGFR designs or 4 TREM2 designs.

The complete target test remains: 89 TREM2 or 378 EGFR designs. The antigen, assay, and design distribution all change together.

What counts as binding?

Source-reported binders and weak binders are B. Reported nonbinders are NB. Failed expression and missing measurements are excluded; no missing Kd is invented.

These labels mean binding detected under the source assay conditions, not necessarily strong binding.

What this cannot establish yet

TREM2's assayed designs were preselected using Boltz-2 ipSAE. Family groups are conservative sequence/scaffold proxies; distant structural relatives may remain.

Assay constructs are not fully verified. Retrospective success does not establish performance on new BCMA designs or adversarial escape mutations.

Training, metric definitions, and reproducibility

Simple models: one regularized logistic model for each score, one combining all four scores, and a binder-length-only control. Standardization is fitted on training data only. C = 1; no class weighting, oversampling, hyperparameter search, or post-hoc calibration. The prevalence control predicts the training fraction of binders.

Raw baselines: ipSAE and ipTM rank larger values first; FoldX and Rosetta rank lower energies first. Raw scores are not probabilities, so Brier scores are not applicable. A one-score logistic model preserves its ranking unless it reverses direction.

AUROC: how often a binder ranks above a nonbinder; 0.5 is chance. Average precision: precision summarized over recall, with the binding prevalence as the constant-score baseline. Brier score: mean squared probability error; lower is better. Compare it with the training-prevalence control.

Family construction: global BLOSUM62 alignment with gap penalties −10 / −0.5; ≥50% aligned identity and ≥80% coverage of both sequences. Embedded domains receive a ≥70% identity / ≥90% shorter-sequence coverage check. Explicit lineage-name connections supplement sequence grouping. Authors and tools alone do not define families.

Scope: EGFR BLI S04 replicate 1 and TREM2 SPR S01 replicate 1. Existing protein-only scores are reused, including direct energies on unrelaxed predicted structures. No structure prediction, relaxation, or glycan modeling was run.

Uncertainty: fold ranges describe sensitivity to the held-out family set; they are not confidence intervals. Related sequences and the small number of positive families prevent strong claims about generalization.
